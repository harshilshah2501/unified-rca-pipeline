§ 01 · Executive summary

Coverage is complete. The edge count gap is a feature, not a defect.

Every concept the three incumbent platforms model is reachable from Fusion SaaS's eleven stored edges plus the metamodel — either directly via an edge type, or via the attributes property bag, or via a computed-edge traversal. The three platforms ship between two and five times as many relationship types because they collapse semantics, evidence, and computation into the type itself.

The thesis, validated

Fusion SaaS's 11 stored edges absorb everything ServiceNow's 53 types, BMC's 20 subclasses, and UCMDB's 25 CITs assert — because we separated three concerns the incumbents have left fused: relationship semantics (the edge type), evidence about the assertion (sources + confidence on the metamodel), and operational truth (computed at query time, not stored).

What the comparison revealed, in three lines

  • Coverage: 11/11 Fusion SaaS stored edges have at least two direct conceptual analogues across the three platforms. No Fusion SaaS edge is an invention without precedent.
  • Compression ratio: ServiceNow's 53 types collapse into our 11 with ~4.8× compression. The bulk of SNOW's "extra" types are direction-flip duplicates (Hosts::Hosted on and Hosted by::Hosts both exist), payload-overloading (DR provided by, Backup provided by), or domain-specific narrowing (Cools::Cooled by) we handle via attributes.
  • Differentiation: Three Fusion SaaS capabilities have no native analogue in any of the three: evidence-source as a first-class edge property, separation of stored from computed truth via traversal patterns, and triplet validation enforced at write-time API rather than as cleanup rules.

§ 02 · Methodology

How a match was scored.

Comparing CMDB relationship vocabularies is treacherous: a name match can hide a semantic divergence (ServiceNow's Depends on is broader than Fusion SaaS's), and a name divergence can hide a semantic exact match (UCMDB composition and our composedOf). The matching ladder used in this document.

VerdictVisualDefinition
Exact EXACT Same lifecycle behavior, same impact propagation, same identity contribution. The two concepts are functionally interchangeable — only the name differs. Example: Fusion SaaS composedOf ↔ UCMDB composition.
Partial PARTIAL Concept matches on two of the three dimensions (lifecycle, impact, identity). Example: SNOW Hosted on covers our runsOn use case but is also used for physical-housing scenarios that Fusion SaaS splits into locatedIn.
Split SPLIT The incumbent's single edge type maps to two or more Fusion SaaS edges — and we believe the split is correct because the use cases have distinct lifecycle or impact behavior. Example: SNOW Depends on covers Fusion SaaS dependsOn + part of communicatesWith + sometimes realizes.
None NONE The platform has no edge type that even partially represents the Fusion SaaS concept. This is rare and is called out as a defensible Fusion SaaS differentiator.

Scope of comparison

  • In scope: stored relationship type definitions, default semantics, lifecycle behavior (cascade / detach / independent), identity participation, impact propagation, evidence sources platforms typically produce these edges from.
  • Out of scope: CI class hierarchies (a different document), TQL / IRE / pattern-matching engines, UI behavior of dependency views, discovery probe inventories.
  • Caveat: ServiceNow allows admins to add custom cmdb_rel_type records freely. The 53-count is the baseline, not a ceiling. Real production instances often hit 70-100.

§ 04 · Per-edge deep dive

For each Fusion SaaS edge — the conceptual lineage and the finding.

For each of the fifteen edges, this section spells out exactly what each platform calls the equivalent concept, where the semantics drift, and what we should do about it (if anything).

Edge 01 · Composition

composedOf

A composedOf B  /  B partOf A
cascade 1:N
B is a structural part of A; B has no meaningful existence outside A.
ServiceNow PARTIAL
Contains::Contained by
SNOW does not distinguish lifecycle-bound parts (CPU inside a server) from logical containment (server inside a rack). Both use Contains. This is a known modeling weakness — cascade-delete behavior must be inferred from the CI class pair, not from the edge type.
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_Component BMC_HostedSystemComponents
BMC_Component is the textbook part-of relation. BMC_HostedSystemComponents is the canonical specialization for a system→hardware-component (CPU, disk, NIC).
UCMDB UDM EXACT
composition
Direct semantic match. UCMDB explicitly documents: "deleting such relationship also removes part CI." Used for node→cpu/disk/memory composition.
Finding
Fusion SaaS's composedOf is the cleaner version of UCMDB's composition — same semantics, same lifecycle. It is strictly better than ServiceNow's overloaded Contains, which forces consumers to infer cascade behavior from CI-class context. No schema change needed.
Edge 02 · Containment

contains

A contains B  /  B containedIn A
detach 1:N
B is logically located inside A but has independent existence; B can be reassigned to another container without ceasing to exist.
ServiceNow SPLIT
Contains::Contained by In Rack::Rack Contains Datacenter::Houses
SNOW conflates logical containment with structural composition (see composedOf). Several physical-class variants (In Rack, Datacenter::Houses) exist but are direction-flip duplicates of Contains.
BMC Helix CDM PARTIAL
BMC_MemberOfCollection BMC_InSegment BMC_InIPSubnet
BMC spreads "containment" semantics across multiple specialized subclasses: BMC_InIPSubnet for IP-in-subnet, BMC_InSegment for network-segment membership. No single direct equivalent.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
containment
UCMDB explicitly distinguishes containment from composition — the part has independent identity, can be reassigned. One-to-one mapping with our split.
Finding
UCMDB validates our composedOf/contains split as a real distinction worth making. ServiceNow's flat Contains is the antipattern we explicitly avoided. BMC's specialization-per-domain approach (InIPSubnet, InSegment) is something we handle via the source/target class triplet — same expressiveness, smaller vocabulary.
Edge 03 · Runtime hosting

runsOn

A runsOn B  /  B runs A
detach identity N:1
A is a runtime instance whose execution is hosted by B's runtime substrate; A's identity is partly defined by where it runs.
ServiceNow SPLIT
Runs on::Runs Hosted on::Hosts Virtualized by::Virtualizes Hosts::Hosted on
SNOW community has documented confusion about which of these to use when — the docs themselves contradict each other on whether DB-on-server is "Runs on" or "Hosted on." Direction-flip duplicates (Hosts::Hosted on AND Hosted by::Hosts) make this worse.
BMC Helix CDM SPLIT
BMC_HostedSystemComponents BMC_Hosted BMC_HostedService
BMC splits by what is hosted: HostedService for service-on-system, HostedSystemComponents for hardware-on-system, generic BMC_Hosted for software-on-system. Conceptually clean but vocabulary-heavy.
UCMDB UDM PARTIAL
execution_environment deployed usage
UCMDB's execution_environment CIT is the closest direct match; deployed is used for application-on-system; usage is the default for non-monitoring CIs when no more specific type applies.
Finding
All three platforms split runtime-hosting into multiple types and the documentation around which to use is famously confusing (ServiceNow community has years of threads on this). Fusion SaaS's single runsOn edge with triplet-validated source/target classes (Process→OS, OS→VM, VM→Hypervisor, Container→K8sNode) is the cleaner model — the class pair encodes what the platforms encode in the edge type.
Edge 04 · Operational dependency

dependsOn

A dependsOn B  /  B usedBy A
independent impact N:M
A requires B's function to operate correctly; if B is degraded or unavailable, A is degraded or unavailable.
ServiceNow SPLIT
Depends on::Used by Used by::Depends on Uses::Used by Consumes::Consumed by
The community has a multi-year unresolved thread on whether "Depends on" and "Consumes" mean the same thing. Direction-flip duplicates (both Depends on::Used by and Used by::Depends on exist in baseline) inflate the count without adding semantics.
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_Dependency
Single dependency type with explicit direction semantics: "If A depends on B, then A is Destination, B is Source." Clean. Maps to CIM_Dependency upstream.
UCMDB UDM SPLIT
dependency client_server db_link
Generic dependency plus protocol-specific narrowings: client_server for client-server protocols, db_link for database links. The narrowings carry protocol-specific attributes we handle via our attributes bag.
Finding
BMC's BMC_Dependency is the cleanest equivalent in the industry. Our dependsOn matches it 1:1. UCMDB's protocol-specific narrowings are something we can absorb via attributes (protocol, port) on the edge — we don't need db_link as a separate edge type. The strategically important finding here: ServiceNow's overloading of Depends on for everything is one of the documented sources of CMDB drift, and our cleaner separation (dependsOn vs. communicatesWith vs. realizes) is defensible.
Edge 05 · Observed communication

communicatesWith

A communicatesWith B
independent N:M
A and B were observed exchanging traffic. This is an evidence claim, not yet a promoted dependency.
ServiceNow SPLIT
Connects to::Connected by Sends data to::Receives data from Exchanges data with::Exchanges data with Talks to
SNOW splits directional ("Sends data to") from symmetric ("Exchanges data with") and physical-connection ("Connects to") into separate types. We unify these via the directional and protocol attributes on a single edge.
BMC Helix CDM NONE
BMC_HostedAccessPoint (indirect)
BMC Discovery infers dependency from traffic but does not store the raw observation separately. The observation collapses into BMC_Dependency at sync time. The intermediate "we saw traffic but haven't yet promoted to dependency" state is invisible.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
talk communication
UCMDB's talk CIT was designed exactly for observed traffic between hosts — distinct from a promoted dependency. Direct conceptual match.
Finding
This is one of Fusion SaaS's strongest semantic wins. BMC simply doesn't model observed-but-unpromoted traffic; the inference collapses into dependency at sync. UCMDB's talk is the closest analogue and validates that the distinction is worth making. By keeping communicatesWith distinct from dependsOn — and tracking the evidence/confidence on each — we can show operators which dependencies are inferred from traffic vs. asserted by IaC or human authorship. This is the foundation for the "promote to dependency" workflow.
Edge 06 · Concrete-to-abstract realization

realizes

A realizes B  /  B realizedBy A
independent N:M
A is the concrete implementation that fulfills the abstract role B (Application realizes ApplicationService realizes BusinessService realizes BusinessCapability).
ServiceNow EXACT
Realized by::Realizes Provided by::Provides Implemented by::Implements
The CSDM "Service Spine" — Business Application → Application Service → Application → Infrastructure — is built on these types. This is ServiceNow's most architecturally-important edge concept.
BMC Helix CDM PARTIAL
BMC_ServiceRealizedByOffering BMC_OfferingMeasuredBy
BMC ships a dedicated realize edge but limits it to the Service→ServiceOffering link. Broader concrete-to-abstract realization (Application→ApplicationService) tends to go through BMC_Dependency, blurring the distinction.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
realization
Direct match. UCMDB uses realization for the service-spine: BusinessService realizedBy InfrastructureService realizedBy RunningSoftware.
Finding
ServiceNow and UCMDB both validate that realizes is its own first-class edge concept — distinct from dependency. BMC's narrower scoping is the weaker model. Our realizes aligns with the dominant pattern. This is the edge that powers criticalPath (computed): traversing from Business Capability down through realize-chains to concrete infrastructure.
Edge 07 · Logical grouping

memberOf

A memberOf B  /  B hasMember A
detach N:M
A is one of several entities that together constitute the group, cluster, or pool B; A retains its own identity and can move between groups.
ServiceNow EXACT
Members::Member of Member of::Members Cluster of::Cluster
Used heavily for cluster nodes, application-service groupings, dynamic CI groups. Direction-flip duplicate exists. SNOW also has a parallel "cluster nodes" class-based approach used by Discovery — two ways to model the same thing, which causes data-quality drift.
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_MemberOfCollection BMC_IPSubnetsInCollection BMC_LNsInCollection BMC_SegmentsInCollection
BMC has the most aggressively specialized membership types — one per CI domain. BMC_MemberOfCollection is the abstract; the rest are CI-class-narrowed subclasses. We absorb the narrowings via the triplet rules.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
membership aggregation
UCMDB's membership is the cluster / group / load-balancer-pool edge. aggregation exists as a separate (UML-inspired) abstract type.
Finding
All three platforms have a direct equivalent. The interesting note is BMC's per-domain specialization (separate BMC_IPSubnetsInCollection, BMC_LNsInCollection, etc.) — this is the "specialize the edge type per CI class" antipattern Fusion SaaS explicitly rejects. We get the same constraint via triplet validation without the vocabulary bloat.
Edge 08 · Control-plane management

manages

A manages B  /  B managedBy A
independent N:M
A is a software or service that has read/write control over B's configuration or lifecycle (vCenter manages ESX host, K8s control plane manages pods, Active Directory manages domain-joined nodes).
ServiceNow SPLIT
Managed by::Manages Managed by group::Manages group Monitored by::Monitors Administered by
SNOW conflates technical control-plane management with human/group ownership ("Managed by group"). Community has documented confusion here. Fusion SaaS splits these: manages is technical, ownedBy is human.
BMC Helix CDM PARTIAL
BMC_HostedService (when management software hosted)
BMC has no first-class "manages" relationship. Management software is modeled as a service hosted on a system, with the managed-to-manager link inferred via dependency. Architecturally weaker than our approach.
UCMDB UDM PARTIAL
monitored_by control_relationship
monitored_by is the canonical observability-related case; control_relationship covers configuration management plane control. Closest direct equivalent.
Finding
Fusion SaaS's split of technical-management from human-ownership is correct and is one of the documented sources of confusion in ServiceNow ("Managed by Group" vs. "Owned by" — see the unresolved Nov 2025 community thread). BMC's absence of a first-class manages edge is a real gap in their model. We're aligned with UCMDB's stronger split.
Edge 09 · Governance accountability

ownedBy

A ownedBy B  /  B owns A
independent N:1
A is the entity (team, person, business unit, cost center) accountable for B's operation, cost, and lifecycle decisions.
ServiceNow EXACT
Owned by::Owns Owns::Owned by Managed by::Manages (when human)
Used for human ownership, business-unit accountability, cost-center attribution. The same problem as manages: SNOW conflates human ownership with technical management when "Managed by" is used for a person/group.
BMC Helix CDM NONE
BMC treats human ownership as a CI attribute (Owner, SupportGroup fields on BMC_BaseElement), not as a relationship to a Person CI. This is a deliberate modeling choice — but means BMC cannot model the "team owns N CIs" inverse query as cleanly.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
ownership
Direct conceptual match: human-or-team accountability for a CI, modeled as a relationship rather than an attribute.
Finding
BMC's choice to model ownership as an attribute rather than a relationship is a legitimate alternative — but it makes the "team accountability dashboard" use case much harder. UCMDB and ServiceNow agree with us that ownership is a relationship, and our split from manages resolves the conflation that plagues SNOW.
Edge 10 · Placement

locatedIn

A locatedIn B  /  B houses A
independent N:1
A is physically or logically placed in B (server in rack, rack in datacenter, datacenter in region, region in cloud, namespace in cluster).
ServiceNow EXACT
Located in::Houses Datacenter::Houses In Rack::Rack Contains
SNOW splits by physical class — separate types for Datacenter, Rack, Site. Fusion SaaS unifies via the source/target class triplet.
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_ElementLocation
Single dedicated location edge — the cleanest of the three. Maps to BMC_BaseElement ↔ BMC_Location.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
location located
Same semantics as BMC and Fusion SaaS.
Finding
Three direct matches. BMC's approach (one edge type) is what we mirror; SNOW's per-physical-class split is what we explicitly avoid. No schema change needed.
Edge 11 · Version genealogy

succeedsLifecycleOf

A succeedsLifecycleOf B  /  B succeededBy A
independent N:1
A is the next-version, replacement, or DR-pair of B; B may continue to exist but A is the operational successor.
ServiceNow PARTIAL
Upgrade of::Upgraded by DR provided by::Provides DR for Migrated from::Migrated to Backup provided by::Provides backup for
SNOW splits version-genealogy from DR-pairing from backup-pairing into separate edge types. Fusion SaaS unifies under one edge with a relation_subtype attribute (version | dr | backup | migration).
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_Genealogy
Direct conceptual match for "this version succeeds that version." DR-pairing and backup-pairing are handled separately in BMC via BMC_Dependency.
UCMDB UDM NONE
UCMDB tracks version history at the CI attribute level (version, build) rather than as a relationship between CI instances. This is a real modeling gap — UCMDB cannot answer "which version replaced which" as a graph traversal.
Finding
BMC validates the concept as a first-class edge. UCMDB's absence of this edge is a documented limitation that surfaces in CI-history reporting. ServiceNow splits the use cases into separate edges; we keep them unified via a subtype attribute, which is a defensible choice but worth revisiting in v0.2 (see Recommendation R4).
Edge 12 · Blast radius (computed)

impactedBy

A impactedBy B  /  B impacts A
computed N:M
If the status of B degrades, A's status will degrade — computed by traversing impact-bearing edges (dependsOn, runsOn, composedOf, realizes).
ServiceNow EXACT (architecturally)
"Impacted Services" view
SNOW also computes impact via cmdb_rel_ci traversal rather than storing impact as a separate edge type. The traversal logic lives in business rules and the BSM map renderer.
BMC Helix CDM EXACT
BMC_Impact (auto-maintained)
BMC moved from storing BMC_Impact (CMDB 7.5) to auto-maintaining it (CMDB 7.6.03+). They converged on our approach: impact is derived from other relationships, not asserted independently.
UCMDB UDM EXACT
Impacted By (Directly) Impacted By (Potentially)
UCMDB has the cleanest version: an explicit "calculated relationship" type with two subtypes for full-vs-potential dependency. This is the architecture Fusion SaaS mirrors.
Finding
All three platforms converge on the same architectural conclusion: impact must be computed, not stored. UCMDB's explicit "Impacted By (Directly)" / "Impacted By (Potentially)" split is something we should consider absorbing — see Recommendation R2.
Edge 13 · Network reachability (computed)

reaches

A reaches B  /  B reachableFrom A
computed N:M
A can communicate with B over the network given current routing, firewall, and segment configuration.
ServiceNow NONE
SNOW does not compute network reachability as a first-class concept. Reachability is delegated to ITOM Visibility / Discovery probes; the CMDB stores the artifacts (interfaces, routes) but does not surface reach as a queryable view.
BMC Helix CDM NONE
Computed by BMC Helix Operations Management at the network-monitoring layer, not by the CDM. CDM stores ports, IP addresses, subnets — reach is not modeled.
UCMDB UDM NONE
No first-class calculated relationship. Can be expressed as a TQL query over communication + InIPSubnet + backbone, but is not pre-packaged.
Finding
Fusion SaaS's reaches as a computed edge is a real differentiator. The fact that none of the three incumbents surface it as a first-class concept is opportunity, not validation gap — operators want to ask "can my LB reach my DB" as a graph query, not by running a tcpdump.
Edge 14 · Co-failure detection (computed)

sharesFailureDomain

A sharesFailureDomain B (symmetric)
computed N:M
A and B both depend on a common upstream point of failure (same hypervisor, same rack, same availability zone, same upstream switch).
ServiceNow NONE
No stored or computed type. Can be derived via custom scripts against the impact graph, but not a first-class concept.
BMC Helix CDM NONE
No equivalent concept in CDM.
UCMDB UDM NONE
No first-class CIT. Would be a derived TQL view, not a packaged calculated relationship.
Finding
Another Fusion SaaS-only concept. The reason this matters: shared failure domain is what operators actually care about for redundancy planning ("are my two replicas on different hypervisors?"). Modeling it as a first-class computed edge — with the failure-domain key as a property — gives operators a direct query, not a multi-step graph traversal.
Edge 15 · Business-to-infra criticality (computed)

criticalPath

A criticalPath B  /  B underpins A
computed 1:N
From business concept A (BusinessCapability, BusinessApplication), B is one of the concrete infrastructure CIs whose failure would degrade A.
ServiceNow NONE
Indirectly accessible through "Business Service" rollup queries and the impact tree, but not surfaced as a typed relationship operators can query.
BMC Helix CDM NONE
Indirectly via BMC_Impact + service mapping; not a typed concept.
UCMDB UDM NONE
Can be expressed as a TQL on the realization + dependency graph; not pre-packaged.
Finding
Fusion SaaS's fourth computed edge is the executive-reporting wedge: every CMDB has the data to answer "what infrastructure underpins our payment capability?" but none of them ships it as a queryable edge. Modeling it as a computed type (over realizes → dependsOn → runsOn) makes it a first-class operation rather than a custom script.

§ 06 · Gaps and wins

What we have that they don't, and where our model needs to harden.

After mapping all 53 + 20 + 25 incumbent concepts into Fusion SaaS, three things they do that we don't (yet) emerged as worth attention — and four things we do that none of them do emerged as defensible differentiators.

Fusion SaaS wins — capabilities none of the incumbents have natively

Win 01 · Evidence as first-class

Every edge carries its evidence source and confidence

None of ServiceNow, BMC, or UCMDB models evidenceSource + confidence + lastObserved + ttl as standard fields on every relationship instance. They track discovery source on the CI but not on the edge. This is the foundation for Fusion SaaS's AI-native maintenance loop — and the reason we can age out stale edges automatically.

Implication: when a customer asks "what dependencies are we asserting that no probe has confirmed in the last 90 days?", we can answer in one query. None of the incumbents can.

Win 02 · communicatesWith ≠ dependsOn

Observed traffic stays distinct from asserted dependency

Only UCMDB's talk CIT comes close to our communicatesWith as a distinct edge type. BMC collapses observed traffic into BMC_Dependency at sync time, losing the observation→assertion provenance. ServiceNow has overlapping types (Sends data to, Connects to, Talks to) but no clear semantic separation from dependency.

Implication: we can power a "promote to dependency" workflow with explicit human review, and we can show operators which dependencies are inferred-only vs. asserted-and-confirmed.

Win 03 · Computed traversals beyond impact

reaches, sharesFailureDomain, criticalPath as first-class computed edges

All three platforms compute (or auto-maintain) impact as a derived concept — we agree there. None of them surfaces reaches (network reachability), sharesFailureDomain (redundancy planning), or criticalPath (business-to-infra) as queryable edge types. These are typically delegated to network monitoring or ad-hoc TQL scripts.

Implication: operators ask these questions constantly ("can my LB reach my DB?", "are my two replicas in the same blast zone?", "what runs my payment capability?"). Packaging them as first-class computed edges turns ad-hoc scripts into one-line queries.

Win 04 · Triplet validation at write-time

Schema-enforced correctness, not after-the-fact cleanup

ServiceNow allows any rel type between any two CIs at write-time; rules are advisory ("suggested relationships") and admins routinely add custom edges. BMC enforces some validity at the IRE/identification level but only for identity-contributing edges. UCMDB has hierarchical type rules but they are not strictly enforced at API write-time.

Implication: Fusion SaaS's triplet allowlist (config-driven, PR-reviewed) is the answer to the "CMDB drift" problem that Gartner attributes to the ~80% failure rate. Edges that violate triplets are rejected, not flagged for cleanup later.

Gaps — capabilities incumbents have that v0.2 should consider

Gap 01 · Impact directness

UCMDB splits "Directly" from "Potentially" impacted

UCMDB's calculated Impacted By has two subtypes that surface as different query results. Our impactedBy currently returns a flat set. For high-stakes incidents, "definitely affected" vs. "might be affected" is an operational distinction worth preserving.

Implication: see Recommendation R2 — extend impactedBy to surface a directness/confidence dimension in its results.

Gap 02 · Federated identity link

BMC ships BMC_FederatedKeyLink for cross-CMDB reconciliation

BMC models the cross-instance identity-correlation as its own edge type (BMC_FederatedKeyLink). Our metamodel handles this via evidenceSource = identity_correlation, but we don't currently store the cross-instance key relationship itself. For multi-CMDB scenarios (very large enterprises that have not yet consolidated), this is a real gap.

Implication: see Recommendation R5 — consider whether identity-correlation across federated sources warrants a separate edge type or stays as evidence metadata.

Gap 03 · Contract/financial linkage

BMC ships BMC_ContractComponent linking contracts to infrastructure

BMC and ServiceNow both model the contract-to-infrastructure link explicitly (BMC via BMC_ContractComponent, SNOW via the contract module + cmdb_rel_ci). Fusion SaaS's ownedBy + memberOf + attributes covers governance ownership but does not cleanly capture the contract→infrastructure binding needed for cost attribution and license compliance.

Implication: see Recommendation R6 — evaluate whether ITAM use cases (contract → CI binding) need a contract-specific edge in v0.2.

§ 07 · Recommendations for v0.2

Six proposed schema changes, with explicit defensibility.

Cross-validation surfaced six candidate changes — none of them invalidates the v0.1 design. Each is scored by whether it adds new semantics (R1, R5, R6), refines existing semantics (R2, R3), or hardens the metamodel (R4). All are proposed as v0.2 deltas, not v1.0-blocking issues.

R · 01

Add an explicit traffic evidence source to the metamodel enum

WhyThe current metamodel lists traffic as an evidence source for communicatesWith, but doesn't distinguish observed-once from sustained-traffic. UCMDB's talk CIT carries observation frequency as a property.
WhatAdd an observation_frequency attribute to communicatesWith with values one_shot · intermittent · sustained · continuous. This makes confidence scoring more accurate and lets us age out one-shot observations faster than sustained ones.
R · 02

Split impactedBy output by directness, mirroring UCMDB

WhyUCMDB's "Directly" vs. "Potentially" split is the cleanest operational distinction in the industry. Our current flat impactedBy returns a single set, which conflates "this WILL fail" with "this MIGHT fail."
WhatWithout adding a new edge type, the impactedBy computed traversal should return results in two buckets: directly (path traverses only impact-bearing edges with confidence ≥ 0.85) and potentially (path includes at least one inferred or low-confidence edge). The metamodel stays the same; only the computed-edge resolver changes.
R · 03

Tighten dependsOn validation triplets to reject overuse

WhyServiceNow's Depends on is famously overloaded — used for everything from infra to identity to monitoring. Without strict triplet rules, our dependsOn drifts into the same antipattern. The community-documented drift in SNOW is the cautionary tale.
WhatPublish an opinionated triplet rule set for dependsOn with explicit "do not use for" guidance: do not use for runtime hosting (use runsOn), do not use for observed communication without promotion (use communicatesWith), do not use for service-spine realization (use realizes). Triplets reject violations at the API boundary.
R · 04

Decide: keep succeedsLifecycleOf unified or split it

WhySNOW splits version-succession, DR-pairing, backup-pairing, and migration into four separate edge types. BMC keeps them unified under BMC_Genealogy. We currently keep them unified with a relation_subtype attribute. Worth one decision-log entry to confirm.
WhatTwo options: (a) keep unified and rely on the relation_subtype attribute (current direction — minimizes vocabulary growth), or (b) split into succeedsVersionOf, drPairOf, and backupPairOf as separate edges (matches SNOW, more queryable). Recommend (a) with a sharper subtype enum: version | dr | backup | migration | rollback.
R · 05

Add an explicit identity_correlated_with stored edge (or keep as metadata)

WhyBMC's BMC_FederatedKeyLink exists because cross-CMDB reconciliation is a real workflow in large enterprises. Our metamodel handles identity-correlation as evidence metadata, but doesn't store the correlation itself as a graph object.
WhatOpen question for the team: do we need a 12th stored edge identity_correlated_with for cross-source federation, or does evidenceSource = identity_correlation on existing edges cover the use case? Recommendation: stay with metadata for v0.2; revisit if federated-CMDB customers raise it.
R · 06

Decide whether ITAM contract-to-CI binding needs its own edge

WhyBMC's BMC_ContractComponent and ServiceNow's contract module both explicitly model contract-to-infrastructure binding. Fusion SaaS's ITAM module will need this. Currently we'd model it as composedOf (contract composedOf line items) + ownedBy (line item ownedBy infrastructure CI) — workable but indirect.
WhatDefer to ITAM module design. If a contract→CI query is core to the cost-attribution use case, consider a 12th stored edge covers (Contract covers CI). For v0.2 of the SKG spec, document as a known forward consideration without adding to the catalog.

What is NOT recommended for v0.2

  • Do not add direction-flip duplicates. The 13 direction-flip duplicates in SNOW's baseline (e.g., both Depends on::Used by AND Used by::Depends on) are a documented source of operator error. Our forward/reverse verb convention in the metamodel is correct.
  • Do not add protocol-specific narrowings as new edge types. UCMDB's client_server, db_link, backbone, layer2_connection are absorbed into communicatesWith + dependsOn via the attributes bag. Adding them as separate types would inflate vocabulary without semantic gain.
  • Do not add per-CI-class membership variants. BMC's BMC_IPSubnetsInCollection, BMC_LNsInCollection, BMC_SegmentsInCollection are exactly the vocabulary explosion we explicitly avoided. The triplet system covers this constraint cleanly.
  • Do not store impactedBy as a materialized edge. BMC moved away from this between CMDB 7.5 and 7.6.03 because keeping a stored impact graph in sync with ground truth is impossible at scale. We were right to design it as computed.
Bottom line

No v0.1 design choice is invalidated by this cross-validation. Six refinements are worth surfacing to the decision log; all are scoped as v0.2 deltas. Most importantly, the four computed edges — which carry our biggest differentiation claims — pass the comparison cleanly: no incumbent ships them as first-class concepts, but two of the three are architecturally converging on the same model (UCMDB calculated relationships; BMC's auto-maintained impact).
