μ ± 1σ in plain English

μ (mu) is the usual average for that metric over the last 7 days. σ (sigma) is how much it normally bounces around day to day.

μ ± 1σ is the “typical range” — roughly the middle 68% of healthy readings. Murphy uses it like a speed limit: if today’s value is outside that band, the entity stays in the candidate set; if it stays inside (like normal_vm CPU), it is pruned and never reaches Gibbs.

Counterfactual clamping uses μ ± 2σ — a stricter “back to clearly normal” target when simulating “what if we fixed entity A?”
